## Caveats that change how to read the results

- **Evaporation, lake precipitation and the closure factor move together.** `evap_gross` is confirmed (USGS: about 45 inches a year over 1931-76), but it, `precip_lake` and `inflow_efficiency` are collinear: the balance is fitted to the published equilibrium elevations, so raising one forces the others to move. The sensitivity (tornado) chart varies each alone, which overstates how much `evap_gross` on its own could move the answer.
- **The starting stock of shepherded water includes in-lake mineral water.** `shepherd_start` is confirmed, but much of it is water the mineral companies agreed not to take from the lake itself, not water delivered down the rivers, so it is not a guide to how fast river shepherding can grow.
- **The lawn chain now reconciles.** With 5,785 sq ft landscaped per home (DWR 2019 Table 4-9, population-weighted) and 371 KAF of lawn water, the implied depth is about 45 inches, against 50 inches measured on average homes (DWR 2019 Table 4-7). Whether 371 KAF is delivered or depleted water is still not settled; DWR now assumes 91% of outdoor water is depleted.
- **Who-uses-what mixes years.** The residential split is published only for 2024 (lawns 408.5 KAF, 96.9% of residential), while the other rows are 2020-24 averages. The non-residential remainder (218.4 KAF) and `mi_depletion` carry that uncertainty, covered by a 320-390 KAF range.
- **The inflow baselines are ratios of three gauges.** The Bear, Weber and Jordan gauges miss ungauged and minor inflow, so only their ratios to the 2000-2025 mean are used, never their totals.
- **A model flaw found during the goal search.** The lawn module raises full fine revenue from any watering ask, however small, and that money buys water rights. A 0.02% ask moved the 2055 lake by 0.6 ft. The optimiser and goal search now ignore asks under 5%; the module itself still has the flaw and should be fixed before anyone relies on a small ask.
