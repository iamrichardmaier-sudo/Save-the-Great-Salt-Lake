# 2B.6 Our assumptions regarding who uses the water

People in the basin consume 2,387 KAF of water a year, and agriculture accounts for 1,584 KAF of it. We count depletion, the water consumed and not returned to the rivers, rather than diversion, because depletion is what the lake loses. These figures set the base each lever works on, but because they are measured rather than chosen, none of them moves the 2055 lake in our sensitivity tests.

## Depletion by user

**What we assume.** The table below, rebuilt from the state's 2026 water budget so that it adds up to the basin total.

| User | Depletion (KAF a year) | Share | In the charge base? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alfalfa and other hay | 1,025 | 43% | Yes |
| Other crops, pasture and incidental losses | 559 | 23% | Yes |
| Residential outdoor (lawns) | 408.5 | 17% | No |
| Industry, commerce and institutions | 218.4 | 9% | Yes |
| Mineral extraction | 136 | 6% | Yes |
| Reservoir evaporation | 27 | 1% | No |
| Residential indoor | 13.1 | 1% | No |
| **Total** | **2,387** | **100%** | 1,938.4 KAF charged |

![Basin depletion by user, KAF a year.](blob/866fc739-25a5)

**Basis.** The state's 2020–2024 averages are agriculture 1,551 KAF, agricultural incidental losses (riparian vegetation along canals and flood-irrigated fields) 33 KAF, municipal and industrial use 640 KAF, mineral extraction 136 KAF and reservoir evaporation 27 KAF, for a total of 2,387 KAF ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). The rows are then split as follows.

- **Hay** is 64.7% of agricultural depletion. The state's agricultural report finds that "most agricultural irrigated lands (by area and depletion volume) in GSL Basin are in Hay/Turf crop group ... nearly all the depletions in this category are alfalfa and grass hay" ([Utah Division of Water Resources, 2026a](https://water.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GSL-BIP_AgWaterOptimizationReport_FINAL_2026-04-21.pdf)). 0.647 × 1,584 = 1,025 KAF.
- **Other crops** are the remainder of agriculture: 1,584 − 1,025 = 559 KAF.
- **Residential use** is known only for 2024: "the watering of residential lawns depleted 408.5 KAF ... outdoor depletions accounted for 96.9% of all residential depletions" ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). Residential use is therefore 408.5 ÷ 0.969 = 421.6 KAF, of which 13.1 KAF is indoor.
- **Industry, commerce and institutions** are the rest of municipal and industrial use: 640 − 421.6 = 218.4 KAF.

**Verdict:** corrected. The old table did not add up to the basin total: it omitted reservoir evaporation, used an indoor figure of 66 KAF found in no source, and put minerals at 152 KAF rather than 136.

**Two cautions.** First, the residential split is for 2024, while the other rows are 2020–2024 averages, so the industry row carries that mismatch; we give it a range of 320 to 390 KAF for minerals and industry together. Second, the state's December 2025 revision of its water budget, which now assumes 91% of outdoor water is depleted, raised total depletion substantially. The earlier method gave lower figures, and our numbers follow the newer one.

## The charge base

The scarcity charge falls on all depletion except household use and reservoir evaporation:

```latex
\text{charge base} = 1{,}584 \text{ (agriculture)} + 354.4 \text{ (minerals, industry, commerce)} = 1{,}938.4 \text{ KAF a year}
```

Households are outside it by design; as tab 2A.7 explains, a city's saving reaches the lake poorly. Reservoir evaporation has no user to charge. **Verdict:** corrected from 1,912 KAF, which was consistent with the old table's errors.

## Agriculture's share in context

Agriculture's two-thirds of depletion is why our proposal looks there first. It is also why the volume figures matter more than they appear to: the hay row, 1,025 KAF, is larger than the 800 KAF a year the lake needs. Even a partial cut to hay water, if it is shepherded to the lake, closes most of the gap.
