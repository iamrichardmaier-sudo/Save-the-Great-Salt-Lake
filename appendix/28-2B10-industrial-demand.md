# 2B.10 Our assumptions regarding industrial demand

We put the water every announced industrial project would need at 19.078 KAF a year, and the jobs they would add at 11,900. Both are display figures: they do not enter the lake calculation, which already subtracts 92 KAF a year for growth in demand of every kind (tab 2B.4). Both are also unsupported. Only about 1.2 KAF of the 19.1 KAF can be traced to a published source, so we list both in Section 3.

## The industrial pipeline

**Water.** We assume 19.078 KAF a year. **Basis.** The research base traces two projects.

- A data-centre campus in Box Elder County would "divert about 1,215 acre-feet of water annually at full buildout ... leaving net depletion of about 987 acre-feet a year" ([Salt Lake Tribune, 2026b](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2026/10/20/kevin-olearys-utah-data-center/)).
- A proposed nuclear lifecycle campus in Tooele would use about 15,000 gallons a day, about 0.017 KAF a year ([Utah Office of Energy Development, 2026](https://energy.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/RFI-High-Level-Booklet.pdf)).

Together these come to about 1.2 KAF a year. The rest of the 19.1 KAF, for Meta's and QTS's Eagle Mountain campuses and for reactors, could not be traced to any published volume. **Verdict:** unsupported.

**Jobs.** We assume 11,900 jobs. The state's booklet for the nuclear campus estimates "5 – 10K permanent jobs ... once fully operational", contingent on federal selection ([Utah Office of Energy Development, 2026](https://energy.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/RFI-High-Level-Booklet.pdf)); data-centre operations add hundreds each. 11,900 is within reach of the upper end, but no source gives it. **Verdict:** unsupported.

## Why it matters little to the lake

Even the full 19.1 KAF would be 0.8% of the basin's 2,387 KAF of depletion and under 2% of what hay consumes. It is a fifth of the 92 KAF a year of growth our model already subtracts from the lake by 2055. If announced projects needed several times more water than we assume, the growth allowance, not the lake, would absorb most of it. New industrial depletion would also pay the scarcity charge while the lake is short of its goal (tab 1.7).

## Where better figures will come from

Utah now requires large data centres to report their water use ([Utah Division of Water Rights, 2026](https://waterrights.utah.gov/data-centers/)). As those reports accumulate, the pipeline figure can be replaced with measured volumes.
