# 1.1 Overview: our proposal

Our proposal raises the Great Salt Lake from 4,189.7 ft today to a goal of 4,198.5 ft, and holds it there. On average inflow the lake first reaches the goal in 2047. In 2055 it stands at 4,198.57 ft, inside a 10th–90th percentile band of 4,194.55 to 4,204.29 ft. The package spends $100M a year of public money from 2027 to 2055 and returns a net economic value of $55.0B over that period. Water moves from the lowest-value use in the basin, irrigated hay at $368 per acre-foot, to the lake, where the same acre-foot is worth $2,440.

## The settings this document describes

Every number in this document comes from one configuration of the model, found by a goal search: the cheapest package that reaches 4,198.5 ft by 2055 on $100M a year. The goal is the bottom of the state's healthy range (4,198 ft) plus half a foot of margin. The state's own simulations put the added inflow needed at 800 KAF a year ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). In our model, 800 KAF a year holds the lake at 4,198.04 ft.

| Lever | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Public budget | $100M a year, 2027–2055 |
| Monitoring and verification | $50M a year, inside the budget |
| Lease-to-own rights | $2M a year; right priced at $6,000 per acre-foot |
| Scarcity charge | $85 per acre-foot to start, with the escalator on |
| Crop levy | $400 per acre-foot applied above 1.5 acre-feet an acre; 90% of revenue to farm transition |
| Mandate | 1,200 KAF a year floor, phased in over 1 year, penalty $0 |
| Repossession | On, capped at 140 KAF a year, 3% interest on instalments |
| Cloud seeding | $1.5M a year |
| Phragmites removal | $25M a year |
| Crop switching | $2.5M a year from the budget, topped up by levy revenue |
| Residential lawns | No watering cut asked |

## Headline results

![Lake level with our proposal and with no package, 2027–2055. The band is the 10th–90th percentile of 120 simulated inflow sequences; the solid lines are the average-inflow runs.](blob/e2d04087-6b14)

The lake responds quickly at first and then levels off, because a larger lake evaporates more (tab 1.2). On average inflow it climbs to 4,194.27 ft by 2030 (band 4,190.69 to 4,199.80 ft) and 4,197.07 ft by 2035 (band 4,193.40 to 4,202.31 ft). It reaches 4,198.10 ft in 2040 (band 4,194.29 to 4,204.07 ft) and the 4,198.5 ft goal in 2047 (band 4,194.59 to 4,204.93 ft). It ends 2055 at 4,198.57 ft on the yearly schedule, or 4,198.53 ft when the same package is held flat (band 4,194.55 to 4,204.29 ft).

Weather matters more than any lever. Across 120 simulated inflow sequences, 51.7% end 2055 inside the healthy range of 4,198 to 4,205 ft and none fall below the 4,190 ft level the state treats as seriously harmful. The median sequence ends at 4,198.70 ft. With no package, the lake settles near 4,190.93 ft (band 4,183.0 to 4,194.66 ft), 63.3% of sequences end below 4,190 ft and 0.8% end healthy (tab 1.10).

By 2055 the package delivers 857 KAF a year of net water to the lake. That is after two pressures the model subtracts: population growth draws 92 KAF a year and a warming climate takes 100 KAF a year from the rivers.

![Where the water comes from in 2055, by lever, before the delivery share is applied.](blob/697a311b-ccb4)

Of the water moved, 69.8% (694 KAF) is bought and shepherded to the lake as state-held rights, 148 KAF comes through the price signals of the charge and levy, and 153 KAF is ordered under the mandate. The four conservation programmes add 204.9 KAF a year between them.

## The full cost, set against the benefit

The public budget is not the cost. Our cost counts the $100M budget, plus farm revenue lost without compensation, plus livestock receipts put at risk when local feed falls. In 2035 that is $452M a year ($100M + $210.3M + $141.8M), against a benefit of $2,221M. In 2055 it is $298M a year ($100M + $56.2M + $141.9M), against $2,091M. The 2055 cost works out at $348 for every acre-foot delivered, below the $368 an acre-foot of hay earns and far below the $2,440 it is worth in the lake.

The net economic value of $55.0B sums, over 2027–2055, the value of the water in the lake less farm revenue lost and livestock receipts at risk, plus the wages of transition jobs.

## Three ways to divide the economy

**Who consumes the water.** Measured by depletion, the water actually consumed rather than diverted, the basin uses 2,387 KAF a year ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). Alfalfa and other hay take 1,025 KAF, other crops and incidental losses 559 KAF, residential lawns 408.5 KAF, industry, commerce and institutions 218.4 KAF, mineral extraction 136 KAF, reservoir evaporation 27 KAF and indoor household use 13.1 KAF. Agriculture is 1,584 KAF, two-thirds of the total.

![Basin depletion by user, KAF a year (2020–24 averages; residential split from 2024).](blob/866fc739-25a5)

**Who gives up water under our proposal.** Agriculture does, almost entirely. The levy frees 148.2 KAF of hay depletion, repossession has taken 549.6 KAF of farm water by 2035, and crop switching and on-farm efficiency are farm programmes. Cities and industry lose almost nothing by 2055. The scarcity charge falls from $87 per acre-foot in 2027 to nearly zero once the lake recovers, and by then it cuts less than 0.1 KAF a year. No lawn-watering cut is asked.

**Who earns the most from each acre-foot.** Hay earns $368 per acre-foot consumed. Mineral extraction on the lake earns $8,315, institutional and government users about $354,000, manufacturing on city water about $1.3M, and commercial businesses about $2.4M. The same acre-foot left in the lake is worth $2,440.

## Jobs

![Jobs lost and gained, statewide, in 2035 and 2055.](blob/e9ad4bdb-f312)

In 2055 the package costs 7,961 field jobs and 1,265 dairy and beef jobs, and adds 666 transition and monitoring jobs and 7,706 lake-economy jobs, for a net of −854. The losses fall in farm counties and the gains around the lake, so the net figure hides a real dislocation (tabs 1.8 and 1.9).

## Alfalfa in brief

Alfalfa is where the water is and where its value is lowest. Hay depletes 1,025 KAF a year, 43% of all human use in the basin, and earns $368 an acre-foot. Utah grows 3.9% of US alfalfa and the basin about 1.8%. In the model, 414,635 acres leave irrigation and 1.78M tons of hay are forgone by 2055, which is 3.6% of the US alfalfa crop. The model counts every acre-foot taken from agriculture as hay, so this overstates the cut to alfalfa itself (tab 5). Most of the water reaches the lake as rights the state holds and shepherds. These are bought from willing sellers, or repossessed and paid for in instalments where the voluntary market falls short. Tab 1.6 treats alfalfa in depth.
