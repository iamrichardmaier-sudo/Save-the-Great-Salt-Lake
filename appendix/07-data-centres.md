# 1.7 Data centres

The one data-centre campus with a published water figure would consume about 987 acre-feet a year, less than 0.1% of what hay consumes in the basin. Every announced industrial project together is put at 19.1 KAF a year in our model, but that total is our own estimate: no project except one has published a volume. Where the research base is silent, we say so and move the claim to Section 3.

## What the research shows

**The Box Elder campus.** The clearest case is a data-centre campus in Box Elder County whose water-right application projects that it "would divert about 1,215 acre-feet of water annually at full buildout ... leaving net depletion of about 987 acre-feet a year" ([Salt Lake Tribune, 2026b](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2026/10/20/kevin-olearys-utah-data-center/)). About four-fifths of what it diverts is consumed; the rest returns to the system.

**Meta and QTS at Eagle Mountain.** Our research found no published annual water volume, in acre-feet, for Meta's Eagle Mountain expansion or for the QTS campus there.

**The Tooele nuclear campus.** The state's request for information on a nuclear lifecycle campus in Tooele estimates water use of about 15,000 gallons a day, or about 0.017 KAF a year, and 5,000 to 10,000 permanent jobs once fully operational, contingent on federal selection ([Utah Office of Energy Development, 2026](https://energy.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/RFI-High-Level-Booklet.pdf)).

**Reporting.** The Utah Division of Water Rights now runs a reporting programme for large data-centre water use ([Utah Division of Water Rights, 2026](https://waterrights.utah.gov/data-centers/)), so measured volumes should become available.

**Public opinion.** A 2026 poll of Utah voters found they put water ahead of data-centre growth ([Noble Predictive Insights, 2026](https://www.noblepredictiveinsights.com/post/utah-voters-put-water-ahead-of-data-center-growth)).

## Water feared and water consumed

The fear is that data centres will drain the lake. On the evidence we have, they consume little of the basin's water. The Box Elder campus's 987 acre-feet a year is 0.04% of the basin's 2,387 KAF of depletion. Our model's figure for the whole announced industrial pipeline, 19.1 KAF a year, would be 0.8% of basin depletion, against 1,025 KAF for hay. Even if that pipeline figure were several times too low, industry would remain a small user next to agriculture.

How much a facility consumes depends on how it is cooled, and our model treats evaporative and closed-loop data centres as different users. The research base does not measure either, and it does not say whether returned water can be treated and sent on to the lake. We list both as predictions in Section 3.

## Output per acre-foot

Industrial users earn far more from an acre-foot than farms do. Our model's figures for proposed facilities run from $46,000 an acre-foot for an evaporatively cooled data centre to $417,000 for a closed-loop one, and $3.5M for QTS, against $368 for hay. A nuclear plant with wet cooling produces about $27,000 of electricity per acre-foot, based on a published water-consumption factor of 672 gallons per megawatt-hour ([Macknick et al., 2012](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/7/4/045802)). Except for the nuclear figure, these rest on no published water volume, so they appear in Section 3 as predictions rather than findings.

## How our proposal handles industrial growth

Our model already takes 92 KAF a year from the lake by 2055 for growth in demand, which covers new industrial and municipal use together, and every lake level in this document is net of it. New industrial depletion falls inside the scarcity-charge base, so while the lake is short of its goal, a new facility pays for each acre-foot it consumes. Because industry earns over a hundred times more per acre-foot than hay, it can afford to pay the charge or to buy water rights from willing sellers, which moves water from low-value to high-value use without taking it from the lake.
