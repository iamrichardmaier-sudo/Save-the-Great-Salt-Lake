# 1.9 Effects at the end (2055)

By 2055 the lake is held at 4,198.53 ft on average inflow (10th–90th percentile band 4,194.55 to 4,204.29 ft), inside the healthy range, and 51.7% of simulated inflow sequences end healthy. The full cost has fallen to $298M a year while the water delivered is worth $2,091M a year. The farm losses remain, but they are smaller and mostly paid for.

## Jobs

| Who | Jobs in 2055 |
| --- | --- |
| Field workers | −7,961 |
| Dairy and beef | −1,265 |
| Transition and monitoring work | +666 |
| Lake economy (minerals, brine shrimp, recreation) | +7,706 |
| **Net** | **−854** |

![Jobs lost and gained, statewide, in 2035 and 2055.](blob/e9ad4bdb-f312)

The lake economy is back to its full 7,706 jobs, so the net loss has halved since 2035, from −1,743 to −854. The geography has not changed. The 9,226 field, dairy and beef jobs lost are in the farm counties, and the 7,706 lake jobs are around the lake. The statewide net hides a shift of work from rural counties to the lake's industries and shore. These farm job figures are likely too high: they apply a farm-wide average of 19.2 jobs per 1,000 acres to hay, which needs less labour than most crops, and they assume all 414,635 acres removed were in production (tab 2B.8).

## Farmland

In 2055, 414,635 acres are out of irrigation and 1.78 million tons of hay are forgone each year, on the model's accounting that treats all agricultural water removed as hay (tab 1.6). Uncompensated farm revenue lost has fallen to $56.2M a year, a quarter of its 2035 level, because by now 694 KAF a year of the water comes from rights the state leases or holds, and only 153 KAF is ordered. Farmers also receive $37.5M a year in crop-switching payments funded by the levy.

## Health

With the package the lake covers 1,465 square miles in 2055, against 963 with no package: 1,299 km² less exposed lakebed. At $81M to $175M a year for every 100 km² ([Harris et al., 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35270)), that avoids $1,052M to $2,273M a year of dust-related health costs, more than the full cost of the package on its own. As in 2035, the research base gives no count of illnesses avoided.

## Minerals, brine shrimp and recreation

On average inflow the lake is above 4,198 ft, the level at which mineral companies receive their full base water rights ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)), though in nearly half of the simulated sequences it is below. At a healthy lake, mineral extraction produces $1,130.8M of output a year and 5,368 jobs, recreation $135.8M and 1,764 jobs, and brine shrimp $56.7M and 574 jobs, all in 2010 dollars ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)).

The full cost is $348 for every acre-foot delivered, less than an acre-foot earns in hay and a seventh of what it is worth in the lake. Summed over 2027–2055, the net economic value of the package is $55.0B. The largest remaining cost is not the budget but the $141.9M of livestock receipts at risk, which arises because the hay cut in our run is larger than the basin's forage surplus; if the true cut to alfalfa is smaller, as tab 1.6 suggests, so is this cost.
