# 2 Our assumptions

Our model rests on 102 figures. Of these, 27 are taken directly from a source, 13 are derived from other figures by a stated formula, and 62 are assumptions: values we chose, most of them bounded or supported by published evidence. Only three have no source of any kind, and they are set out in Section 3. This section explains every figure, in two parts, each ordered by how much the figure moves the lake level in 2055.

## How this section is organised

**Part A, the levers (tabs 2A.1–2A.7),** covers what a legislature would set: the mandate, the scarcity charge, the conservation programmes, lease-to-own agreements, the crop levy, the budget and residential lawns. **Part B, the constants (tabs 2B.1–2B.11),** covers what the model relies on but nobody sets: the lake's water balance, river inflow, delivery, climate and growth, crop water use, who uses the water, its value, jobs, public opinion, industrial demand and the variability of inflow.

Each tab tells the story of its assumptions: the value, where it comes from and how it was checked, the economics where the assumption is a market, and the closest real-world analogue where one exists. Each assumption has its own sub-heading, which tab 1.5 links to.

## How each figure was checked

Every figure was re-checked at source in October 2026, and each carries a verdict:

| Verdict | Figures | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed | 19 | The source states the value, or it follows by stated arithmetic |
| Corrected | 24 | A source gave a different value; the model now uses it |
| Range revised | 9 | The value holds; its plausible range was changed |
| Derived | 1 | Worked out from other checked figures |
| Evidence | 33 | An assumption with published studies that support or bound it |
| Unsupported | 8 | Labelled as sourced, but the figure was not found; now treated as an assumption |
| No evidence | 8 | An assumption with nothing published behind it after a real search |

Where we draw on a historical analogue, we rate how closely it matches our situation on a scale of 1 to 5. A 5 is the Great Salt Lake's own measured data; a 4 is the same mechanism in Utah or a closely comparable place; a 3 is the same mechanism elsewhere with important differences; a 2 is a related mechanism; a 1 is a search that found nothing.

## Which assumptions matter most

![How much each figure moves the 2055 lake level, from the low to the high end of its plausible range, one figure at a time.](blob/718a0ad9-d6f2)

We moved each figure from the low to the high end of its plausible range, one at a time, and recorded how far the 2055 lake level moved. The twelve that matter most are:

| Figure | Swing in the 2055 level (ft) | Tab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross evaporation | 4.23 | 2B.1 |
| Precipitation on the lake | 2.88 | 2B.1 |
| River inflow now | 1.90 | 2B.2 |
| Growth of shepherding capacity | 1.85 | 2B.3 |
| The closure factor | 1.50 | 2B.1 |
| The delivery share | 1.43 | 2B.3 |
| Streamflow decline by 2055 | 1.41 | 2B.4 |
| Delivery of ordered water | 0.72 | 2A.1 |
| Growth in demand by 2055 | 0.61 | 2B.4 |
| Most measurement can add to the delivery share | 0.50 | 2B.3 |
| Speed of the charge thermostat | 0.38 | 2A.2 |
| Monitoring money that buys most of the delivery gain | 0.37 | 2B.3 |

The lake's own water balance moves the answer far more than any lever. Two cautions apply. First, gross evaporation, precipitation and the closure factor were fitted together to the state's published lake levels, so raising one would force the others to move; varying each alone overstates its effect. Second, these swings are for the average-inflow run; the weather itself moves the 2055 level across a band of about 10 ft (tab 2B.11).

Most of the levers move the 2055 level very little, because the package is limited by how much water willing sellers will offer and how much can be shepherded, not by any one setting. That is why the ordering in Part A starts with the mandate and the charge, which act on that limit, and ends with the budget and lawns, which barely move the result.
