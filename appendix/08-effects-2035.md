# 1.8 Effects during the process (2035)

By 2035, eight years in, the lake stands at 4,197.07 ft on average inflow (10th–90th percentile band 4,193.40 to 4,202.31 ft), close to the healthy range. Getting there costs $452M a year in all, and the water delivered is worth $2,221M a year. The pain is concentrated: this is the year when the farm losses are largest relative to the gains, and when the jobs lost and gained are furthest apart.

## Jobs

| Who | Jobs in 2035 |
| --- | --- |
| Field workers | −7,949 |
| Dairy and beef | −1,264 |
| Transition and monitoring work | +666 |
| Lake economy (minerals, brine shrimp, recreation) | +6,804 |
| **Net** | **−1,743** |

![Jobs lost and gained, statewide, in 2035 and 2055.](blob/e9ad4bdb-f312)

About 9,200 farm and ranch workers would need new work. The jobs gained are not in the same places. Field, dairy and beef losses fall in the farm counties of the basin, such as Box Elder, Cache and Utah counties, while lake-economy jobs gather around the lake's mineral works, its brine shrimp harvest and the recreation on its shores. A statewide net of −1,743 therefore understates the dislocation: a farm county can lose hundreds of jobs while the state as a whole loses few. Lake-economy jobs reach 6,804 of their 7,706 total in 2035, because the lake is not yet fully recovered.

## Farmland

By 2035 the package has already taken almost all the farmland it will take. Field job losses are within a few dozen of their 2055 level, when 414,635 acres are out of irrigation and 1.78 million tons of hay are forgone each year. As tab 1.6 explains, the model counts all agricultural water removed as hay, so the true cut to alfalfa is smaller. Uncompensated farm revenue lost is $210.3M a year, the highest it will be, because the mandate still orders 571 KAF a year and repossession has taken 549.6 KAF. That falls to $56.2M by 2055 as bought water replaces ordered water.

## Health

With the package, the lake covers 1,342 square miles in 2035, against 957 with no package. That is 998 km² less exposed lakebed. At $81M to $175M a year in health damage for every 100 km² of exposed lakebed ([Harris et al., 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35270)), the package avoids $808M to $1,746M a year of dust-related health costs. The research base gives these costs in dollars only; it holds no count of illnesses, hospital visits or deaths avoided, which we list as a gap in tab 5.

## Minerals, brine shrimp and recreation

At 4,197.07 ft the lake is still just under 4,198 ft, the level at which mineral companies receive their full base water rights ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). At a healthy lake the three lake industries produce $1,130.8M (minerals), $135.8M (recreation) and $56.7M (brine shrimp) of output a year in 2010 dollars ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)). By 2035 they support 6,804 jobs.

## The whole account

| Item | $M a year, 2035 |
| --- | --- |
| Public budget | 100.0 |
| Farm revenue lost, uncompensated | 210.3 |
| Livestock receipts at risk | 141.8 |
| **Full cost** | **452.0** |
| **Benefit: 910 KAF delivered to the lake at $2,440** | **2,221.4** |

The full cost is four and a half times the public budget. Even so, the benefit is almost five times the full cost. Water users also pay $41.6M in crop levy and $17.7M in scarcity charge in 2035. These are transfers rather than costs to the economy, and most of the levy returns to farmers through crop switching.
