# 3 Our predictions

These are the figures we had to set with no research behind them. Three of the model's 102 figures have no source of any kind; the rest of this list is figures we used in the text that the research base does not support. Each line says why we set it as we did. Every other assumption in the model is explained, with its evidence, in Section 2.

## Figures with no source

- **The public budget: $100M a year, 2027–2055.** Set by our brief as the constraint. It is the right order of magnitude: the Legislature put over $500M into water in 2023, mostly one-time, and households say they would pay about $153.6M a year for the lake.
- **Money set aside for lease-to-own: $2M a year.** Chosen by the goal search. Ownership does not change how much water reaches the lake before 2055, so the search left this line low and spent the money on monitoring and programmes.
- **Number of simulated inflow sequences: 120.** A modelling choice: enough for a stable 10th and 90th percentile, accurate to about ±2.7 percentile points, without slowing the interactive model. The state uses 1,000 for its own simulations.

## Figures used in the text without support

- **Water for announced industrial projects: 19.078 KAF a year.** Only about 1.2 KAF traces to published figures. We kept the total as a generous allowance, a fifth of the 92 KAF a year of growth the model already subtracts, so that underestimating it would not flatter the lake.
- **Jobs from those projects: 11,900.** Set near the upper end of the state's 5,000 to 10,000 permanent jobs for the nuclear campus, plus data-centre operations. It is a display figure and enters no result.
- **Water consumed by individual data centres, and output per acre-foot.** Our figures of $46,000 an acre-foot for an evaporatively cooled data centre, $417,000 for a closed-loop one, $758,000 for Meta's Eagle Mountain expansion, $3.5M for QTS, $1.2M for the Tooele fuel-cycle campus and $8.4M for dry-cooled nuclear power rest on no published water volume. We set them by analogy with the published nuclear water factor and with the much higher output per acre-foot of commercial and manufacturing users, on the reasoning that data centres, like those users, consume little water for the value they produce.
- **Whether a data centre's returned water can be treated and sent on to the lake.** Not covered by the research base. We expect closed-loop facilities to return most of what they draw, as the one filed campus returns about a fifth of its diversion, but whether that water reaches the lake depends on where it is discharged.
- **The effect of our hay cut on national hay prices.** The research base has no elasticity of hay demand or supply. We expect the price effect to be small, because the cut is 1.8% to 3.6% of national alfalfa supply and Utah prices already swing by a quarter in a single year, but we cannot put a number on it.
- **Hemp's water use and market.** The hemp figures in tab 1.6 are new research taken from search summaries we could not open. We use them only to explain why our crop-switching programme is costed on wheat rather than hemp.
