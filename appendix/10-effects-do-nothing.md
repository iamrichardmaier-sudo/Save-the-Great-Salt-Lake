# 1.10 Effects if we do nothing

With no package, the lake settles near 4,190.93 ft by 2055 on average inflow (10th–90th percentile band 4,183.0 to 4,194.66 ft). In 63.3% of simulated inflow sequences it ends below the 4,190 ft serious-harm level, and in only 0.8% does it end healthy. Doing nothing costs no public money and no farm jobs, but it gives up about $2,091M a year of value in the lake and leaves a real chance that much of the lake dries out.

## Jobs

| Who | Jobs in 2055, no package |
| --- | --- |
| Field workers | 0 |
| Dairy and beef | 0 |
| Transition and monitoring work | 0 |
| Lake economy | 1,135 |

No farm jobs are lost, and no transition jobs are created. The model shows 927 lake-economy jobs in 2035 and 1,135 in 2055 even with no package, because the lake partly recovers after dry years; this is a feature of the model's rebound, not a forecast that the lake economy grows (tab 5). Against the package, doing nothing keeps about 9,200 farm and ranch jobs in the farm counties and forgoes about 6,600 lake-economy jobs around the lake.

## Farmland

No land leaves irrigation and no hay is forgone. Farm and ranch incomes carry on as they are, so long as the water supply holds. In the dry scenarios below, it may not: a falling lake does not protect upstream irrigators from drought.

## Health

With no package the lake covers about 963 square miles in 2055, against 1,465 with the package, leaving about 1,299 km² more lakebed exposed. At $81M to $175M a year for every 100 km² ([Harris et al., 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35270)), that extra exposed lakebed costs $1,052M to $2,273M a year in dust-related health damage. As elsewhere, the research base gives this in dollars only, not in illnesses.

## Minerals, brine shrimp and recreation

The lake stays below 4,198 ft in almost every sequence, so mineral companies would not receive their full base water rights ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). The lake industries, which at a healthy lake produce $1,130.8M (minerals), $135.8M (recreation) and $56.7M (brine shrimp) a year in 2010 dollars and 7,706 jobs ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)), would operate on a much smaller lake.

## The lake drying out

![Lake level with no package under four dry scenarios: the 10th-percentile path of 120 simulated inflow sequences.](blob/f0d6c4bf-1167)

The average path hides the danger. The lake does not just stop rising; in dry sequences it keeps falling. We ran the no-package case under four drier futures, each with 120 inflow sequences.

| Scenario, no package | Lake in 2055, driest 1 in 10 (ft) | Share of sequences below 4,190 ft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2000–2025 average inflow | 4,183.00 | 63% |
| The driest recent decade (2013–2022) repeated | 4,180.46 | 81% |
| A warming trend | 4,179.69 | 88% |
| A long drought | 4,180.35 | 66% |
| A warming trend and a long drought together | 4,177.48 | 92% |

In the worst case, one sequence in ten leaves the lake at 4,177.48 ft in 2055, more than 12 ft below today. Its surface would shrink from about 929 square miles today to about 534, losing over two-fifths of its area and exposing that lakebed to the wind. Even in the median sequence of that scenario the lake ends at 4,183.55 ft.

Our brief asked us to cite a widely reported warning that the lake could disappear within about five years. That paper is not in our research base, so we do not cite it; we describe the risk from our own model instead and list the missing citation in tab 5.

Doing nothing is not free. Against our package it forgoes about $2,091M a year of value in the lake, to save $298M a year in public money, farm revenue and livestock receipts. Measured separately, its extra exposed lakebed costs $1,052M to $2,273M a year in health damage.
