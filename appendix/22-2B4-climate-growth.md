# 2B.4 Our assumptions regarding climate and growth

We assume two pressures work against the lake over the plan: a warming climate takes 100 KAF a year from the rivers by 2055, and a growing population draws 92 KAF a year more. Both rise gradually; by 2035 they take 28.6 and 26.3 KAF a year. Every lake level in this document is net of them. Neither figure is stated in any source, so both are assumptions, but each is bounded by published evidence. Streamflow decline is the seventh most sensitive figure in our model, moving the 2055 lake by 1.41 ft across its range; growth moves it by 0.61 ft.

## Streamflow decline

**What we assume.** By 2055, a warming climate removes 100 KAF a year from the inflow the lake would otherwise receive. **Basis.** A 2013 study of the Salt Lake City watersheds found that "the stream flow would decline by 1.8 to 6.5 percent for each degree of temperature rise, with an average reduction of 3.8 percent" ([University Corporation for Atmospheric Research, 2013](https://news.ucar.edu/10534/rising-temperatures-challenge-salt-lake-citys-water-supply)). As an illustration, about 2 °F of further warming by 2055 at that rate would cut 3.6% to 13% from the headwaters; applied to roughly 1,665 KAF of lake inflow that is 60 to 215 KAF a year. Our 100 KAF sits in that range. The state's reports give no quantitative forecast of future streamflow. **Verdict:** unsupported as a stated figure; an assumption with a range of 0 to 300 KAF.

This is a gradual trend, separate from the warming baseline in tab 2B.2, which assumes 4.7 °F of warming already in place from the start and cuts inflow by 18%.

## Growth in demand

**What we assume.** By 2055, population and economic growth add 92 KAF a year to municipal and industrial depletion. **Basis.** Utah's households are projected to grow from 1.2 million in 2025 to 2.3 million in 2065 ([Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, 2025](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2025/11/19/utah-population-projected-reach-over-5-million-2065-growth-change/)). Yet municipal and industrial depletion in the basin "have not changed substantially since 1989": after peaking at 385 KAF in 2020, they fell to 325 KAF in 2023 ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2025](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/GSL-Jan2025.pdf)). Our 92 KAF is 28% of the 2023 figure, roughly what a 28% rise in basin population by 2055 would add at constant use per person. No source projects it. **Verdict:** unsupported; an assumption with a range of 40 to 180 KAF.

Growth in demand also covers new industrial users such as data centres (tab 1.7). The announced industrial pipeline we track, 19.1 KAF a year, is a fifth of it.
