# 2A.6 Our assumptions regarding the budget and its split

We assume $100M a year of public money from 2027 to 2055, $2,900M in all, with half of it spent on monitoring and verification. The $100M is a constraint from our brief, not a sourced figure, and we list it in Section 3. It is the right order of magnitude for a sustained state programme, and Utah households say they would pay more than this for a healthy lake. Within the range we tested, the size of the budget barely moves the 2055 lake, because willing sellers, not money, are the binding limit.

## The budget

**What we assume.** $100M a year, every year, from 2027 to 2055. **Basis.** A policy constraint, not a measurement. For scale: in its 2023 General Session the Legislature "funded over $500 million for water conservation, development and infrastructure", including $200 million for agricultural water optimisation ([Utah State Senate, 2023](https://senate.utah.gov/?p=2865)), most of it one-time money. Surveys suggest Utah households would pay $128 a year each for a recovered lake, about $153.6M a year statewide (tab 2B.9). A sustained $100M a year is therefore within what the state has spent and what its residents say they would pay.

**Why more money does not buy much more lake.** With the mandate switched off, we ran the model at budgets from $25M to $800M a year.

| Budget ($M a year, mandate off) | Net water to the lake in 2055 (KAF a year) | Lake level in 2055, average inflow (ft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | 774.9 | 4,197.50 |
| 50 | 789.0 | 4,197.59 |
| 100 | 815.6 | 4,197.75 |
| 200 | 863.4 | 4,198.07 |
| 300 or more | 869.9 | 4,198.11 |

Quadrupling the budget from $25M to $100M adds 0.25 ft. Beyond $300M nothing changes, because every willing seller has already sold. These are average-inflow runs; across simulated weather each carries a band about 10 ft wide, as the package does (4,194.55 to 4,204.29 ft in 2055), so these differences are small next to the weather. That is why our package spends its money where it changes how much water arrives, on monitoring and programmes, and relies on a mandate as a backstop for the last half foot.

## How the budget is split

| Line | $M a year (setting) | $M over 2027–2055 (schedule) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monitoring and verification | 50 | 1,830.6 |
| Phragmites removal | 25 | 553.0 |
| Water rights and lease-to-own | 2 | 289.1 |
| On-farm efficiency | 19 | 115.1 |
| Crop switching (budget share; the levy adds more) | 2.5 | 72.1 |
| Cloud seeding | 1.5 | 40.0 |
| **Total** | **100** | **2,900** |

The split came from the goal search: the package that reaches 4,198.5 ft by 2055 on $100M a year. The yearly schedule differs from the flat settings because it front-loads monitoring: in 2027 the whole $100M goes to monitoring, and the programmes start in 2028. Nothing is left unspent in any year.

### Monitoring and verification

**What we assume.** $50M a year, the top of the 0 to $50M range. **Basis.** A policy choice. Actual Utah spending on water measurement is far smaller: the Legislature appropriated $1M of one-time and $1M of ongoing money for measurement in the basin, and federal agencies added $3M ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2025](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/GSL-Jan2025.pdf)); a 2025 request sought $6.3M one-time and $3.6M ongoing ([Utah Legislature, 2024](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2024/pdf/00001081.pdf)). Our $50M is therefore many times observed spending: a well-funded shepherding and verification programme, not an observed cost. The goal search put it at its maximum because, in our model, monitoring raises both the share of water that arrives and the volume that can be shepherded, and those limits bind before money does (tab 2B.3). **Verdict:** evidence; a policy choice.

### Lease-to-own money

**What we assume.** $2M a year. Whatever this line does not absorb flows to the conservation programmes. No source states a lease-to-own budget, and the lake's water trust works through one-year, multi-year and permanent transactions funded from a one-time $40M appropriation in 2022 ([Great Salt Lake Watershed Enhancement Trust, 2026](https://media.audubon.org/2026-04/GSLWET_Impact_Report_2025.pdf)). We list it in Section 3. Tab 2A.4 explains what it means for ownership.
