# 5 Contradictions and gaps

Where two parts of our research disagreed, we chose one value and used it everywhere in this document. The first list records each disagreement and how we resolved it. The second records what the document needed and the research base did not have.

## Gaps

- **The "lake could disappear in five years" paper.** Our brief asked us to cite it. It is not in the research base, so we did not, and described the risk from our own model instead (tab 1.10). The team should supply the citation if it is to be used.
- **Illness counts.** The research base gives health effects in dollars only: $81M to $175M a year for every 100 km² of exposed lakebed. It has no counts of illnesses, hospital visits or deaths avoided. A University of Utah estimate puts current annual health costs near $115M, far below the $1.1B to $2.3B of the working paper we use; that difference is unresolved.
- **The elasticity of hay demand and supply.** Not in the research base, so the effect of our cut on hay prices is a prediction, not a finding.
- **A national average for alfalfa water use per acre.** The research base holds Utah figures only.
- **Data-centre water volumes.** No project except one Box Elder campus publishes a volume; Meta and QTS publish none. Utah's new reporting rule should fill this gap.
- **The delivery share of shepherded water, and of ordered water.** No published measurement exists of how much dedicated water reaches a terminal lake, or how much less ordered water does. These are among the most sensitive numbers in our model.
- **The lawn-fine flaw.** The model's lawn module raises full fine revenue from any watering ask, however small, and spends it on water rights. A 0.02% ask moved the 2055 lake by 0.6 ft. Our package asks for no cut, so our results are unaffected, but the module should be fixed.
- **Sources that block automated access.** Two Wiley journal articles (the irrigation demand meta-analysis and the outdoor watering restrictions study) block bots and were read through other copies. The EarthRef record of the meta-analysis fails under one fetch method, and the Water Online summary of a 2025 study returned a server error.
- **HB 410.** The enrolled bill text was not opened. Our reading, that its two-in-five limit applies to temporary leases and not to lease-to-own acquisition, should be checked against it.
- **New research on hemp.** The container this document was written in could not open web pages, so the hemp figures in tab 1.6 come from search summaries and should be checked at source.
- **The hosted model.** The published, interactive version of our model could not be found, so this document cites the model's data export rather than a live link.
- **Birds and wetlands.** The lake's value to migratory birds is not modelled or counted in any figure.
