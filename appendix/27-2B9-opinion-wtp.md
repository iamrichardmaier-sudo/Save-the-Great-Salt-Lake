# 2B.9 Our assumptions regarding public opinion and willingness to pay

Utahns are worried about the lake, will pay to save it, and prefer incentives to penalties. We assume households would pay $128 a year each, $153.6M a year statewide, and that 58% of voters prefer incentives while 22% prefer penalties. These figures shape the design of our package, which pays for water rather than fining for it, and they score how six groups would view it. None changes the lake level.

## Polling

**What we assume.** Asked for the best state approach to cosmetic water use, 58% of Utah voters prefer incentives for water-efficient landscaping, and 22% prefer restrictions with penalties. **Basis.** A 2026 poll of 800 Utah voters for the Deseret News and the Hinckley Institute of Politics found that "more than half of state voters (58%) prefer state solutions that incentivize water-efficient landscaping, while 22% think the state should impose restrictions with penalties on cosmetic water use (like watering lawns)" ([Deseret News & Hinckley Institute of Politics, 2026](https://www.deseret.com/politics/2026/05/27/poll-utahns-concerned-about-water-great-salt-lake/)). Penalties lost in every subgroup measured; the most supportive group, the unemployed, favoured them at 28%. **Verdict:** confirmed; the poll was originally attributed to a different pollster and has been re-attributed.

Concern is broad. Polls in 2026 found 88% of rural residents concerned about the lake, higher than urban residents; 78% of Democrats but only 36% of Republicans very concerned; and 81% of all Utah voters backing a federal funding package for the lake ([Deseret News & Hinckley Institute of Politics, 2026](https://www.deseret.com/politics/2026/05/27/poll-utahns-concerned-about-water-great-salt-lake/); [Noble Predictive Insights, 2026](https://www.noblepredictiveinsights.com/post/utah-voters-put-water-ahead-of-data-center-growth)). Voters also put water ahead of data-centre growth.

## Support scores

We score how six groups would view a package by weighing four things: how much of the water is bought rather than ordered, how much the lake recovers, what happens to farm income, and the public cost. The weights are our own judgement, set from the polling above, not themselves surveyed.

| Group | Weight on bought water | Weight on the lake | Weight on farm income | Weight on cost | Score for our package |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Farmers association | 42 | 10 | 30 | −4 | 87.8 |
| Citizens in rural areas | 30 | 26 | 20 | −6 | 83.1 |
| Citizens in metropolitan areas | 24 | 40 | 6 | −8 | 85.0 |
| State office of commerce | 26 | 28 | 10 | −14 | 78.1 |
| Republican legislators | 46 | 18 | 20 | −12 | 80.3 |
| Democratic legislators | 14 | 52 | 8 | −4 | 100 |

The average is 86, and the weakest group is the state office of commerce, at 78.1, because it weighs the public cost most heavily. Farmers and Republican legislators weigh bought water heavily and dislike being ordered, which is why a package that buys 69.8% of its water scores well with them. These are modelled scores, not survey results, and they should be read as a check on design rather than a forecast of votes. **Verdict:** the poll facts behind them are confirmed; the weights are assumptions.

## Willingness to pay

**What we assume.** A Utah household would pay $128 a year to save the lake. **Basis.** A contingent-valuation study by Weber State University economists found that respondents "who were moderately sure their financial contributions would make a difference were willing to pay between $113 and $144 per household each year" ([Salt Lake Tribune, 2026a](https://www.sltrib.com/news/environment/2026/10/16/most-utahns-are-willing-open-their/)). Our $128 is the midpoint. Across all levels of certainty and ways of paying, the study's estimates run from $36.56 to $485.10, so the moderate band is a cautious middle. The manuscript is under peer review and not yet public, so this rests on press coverage. **Verdict:** confirmed.

**Statewide.** Utah had about 1.2 million households in 2025 ([Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, 2025](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2025/11/19/utah-population-projected-reach-over-5-million-2065-growth-change/)).

```latex
\$128 \times 1.2 \text{ million households} = \$153.6\text{M a year}
```

The press coverage gives "up to $162 million per year across the state's 1.1 million households", which uses an older household count. **Verdict:** households corrected from 1,265,625, which had been worked backwards from the $162M.

**How we use it.** Willingness to pay is not part of our benefit figure, which uses the lake's value of $2,440 an acre-foot (tab 2B.7). It is evidence that a sustained public budget of $100M a year is within what residents say they would pay.
