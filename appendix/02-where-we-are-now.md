# 1.2 Where we are now

The Great Salt Lake stood at 4,189.7 ft at the end of water year 2026, below the 4,190 ft level the state treats as seriously harmful and more than 8 ft under the bottom of its healthy range ([U.S. Geological Survey, 2026a](https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/dv/?format=json&sites=10010000&parameterCd=62614&startDT=2025-09-01&endDT=2026-10-23)). People take more water from its rivers than the lake can spare. Every foot of recovery costs more water than the foot below it.

## A lake with no outlet

The Great Salt Lake is a terminal lake: rivers flow in and nothing flows out except by evaporation. Its level is the balance between what arrives and what the sun takes. When inflow falls, the lake shrinks until its smaller surface evaporates only as much as now arrives, and then it settles at a lower level. When inflow rises, it grows until the larger surface evaporates the extra.

The amounts are large. From 1931 to 1976 the lake lost an average of 2.9 million acre-feet a year to evaporation, about 45 inches of water off its surface ([Arnow, 1978](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1978/0912/report.pdf)). Its three main rivers, the Bear, the Weber and the Jordan, delivered an average of 1,665 KAF a year between 2000 and 2025 ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). Before reaching the lake, people in the basin deplete 2,387 KAF a year: water consumed by crops, lawns, industry and evaporating reservoirs, and not returned to the rivers. Two-thirds of that, 1,584 KAF, is agriculture ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)).

Weather swings the inflow hard from year to year. Flow from the three rivers fell below 1,000 KAF in 2021 and 2022, rebounded above 2,000 KAF in 2023 and 2024, then fell to 1,281 KAF in 2025 ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). A wet year can lift the lake by feet; a run of dry years undoes it.

## How far it has fallen

The state's healthy range is 4,198 to 4,205 ft ([Office of the Great Salt Lake Commissioner, 2024](https://greatsaltlake.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/Great-Salt-Lake-Strategic-Plan.pdf)). The Commissioner asks for a contingency plan to keep the south arm above 4,190 ft "to avoid serious adverse effects". Today's 4,189.7 ft is below that floor. At that level the lake covers about 929 square miles; at the bottom of the healthy range it would cover about 1,423 ([Root, 2023](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/6467b42fd34ec11ae4a8afb1)). The difference, about 500 square miles, is lakebed open to the wind.

If inflow simply continues at its 2000–2025 average, the lake settles near 4,191 ft and goes no higher. The state's own simulations put the added inflow needed to reach 4,198 ft by 2055 at 800 KAF a year ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)). That is about a third of everything the basin now consumes.

## What is at stake

**Dust and health.** Exposed lakebed blows fine dust over the cities of the basin, home to 86% of Utahns ([Utah Population Committee, 2025](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/UPC-State-and-County-Population-Estimates-with-Components-of-Change-2020-2025.xlsx)). A 2026 study of the lake estimates that every 100 km² of exposed lakebed costs $81M to $175M a year in health damage, or $1.1B to $2.3B a year at today's exposed area ([Harris et al., 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35270)).

**Minerals.** Companies extract minerals from the lake's brine. In 2010 dollars the mineral sector produced $1,130.8M of output, including indirect and induced effects, and supported 5,368 jobs ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)). The companies' full base water rights depend on the lake staying at or above 4,198 ft ([Great Salt Lake Strike Team, 2026](https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/GSL-Summary-Mar2026.pdf)).

**Brine shrimp.** The harvest of brine shrimp eggs for aquaculture produced $56.7M of output and 574 jobs ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)).

**Recreation and tourism.** Recreation on and around the lake produced $135.8M of output and 1,764 jobs ([Utah Legislature, 2022](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2022/pdf/00004675.pdf)).

**Birds.** The lake's wetlands and shoreline are a stopover for migratory birds. We did not model them, and they are not counted in any figure in this document.

In total the lake economy supported 7,706 jobs. One economic study for the state put the cost of a declining lake at $1.69B to $2.17B a year ([Audubon, 2019](https://www.audubon.org/news/potential-costs-declining-water-levels-great-salt-lake-could-be-billions)).

## Why each foot costs more than the last

The lake is shaped like a shallow dish. As it rises, it spreads over flat ground, so its surface grows faster than its depth. A larger surface evaporates more, so the inflow needed to hold the lake at a level rises steeply with the level.

![Inflow needed to hold the lake at each level: area at that level times net evaporation of 2.52 ft a year, on the USGS elevation-area table.](blob/fa92803c-83f0)

To hold the lake at 4,190 ft takes about 1,621 KAF a year. To hold it at 4,198 ft takes about 2,459 KAF a year, and at 4,205 ft about 3,475 KAF. Going from 4,190 to 4,191 ft costs about 47 KAF a year of extra inflow; going from 4,197 to 4,198 ft costs about 149 KAF. The table shows the same thing in terms of added inflow: the first 200 KAF a year raises the lake by about 2.6 ft, while the last 200 KAF before the goal raises it by about 1.3 ft.

| Inflow added to today's average (KAF/yr) | Level the lake settles at (ft) |
